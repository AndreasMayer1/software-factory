## 4. Exact YAML Schema for `scope_exclusions`

### 4.1 Persona-level schema

Position: Append as the last YAML field in persona frontmatter, after the `pcd` block.

```yaml
scope_exclusions:
  - area: "Brief description of the excluded use case or scenario category"
    reason: technical | effort | business | strategic
    reason_detail: "Optional explanation of why this is excluded"
    reconsider_in: "v2.0"  # optional — version or milestone when to reconsider
```

**Reason taxonomy** (from goal.md):
- `technical` — Not feasible with current technology or architecture
- `effort` — Feasible but too expensive relative to value for current scope
- `business` — Business/market decision (not relevant to our target users)
- `strategic` — Strategic decision to defer (e.g., MVP scope, legal risk avoidance)

**Rules for persona-level exclusions**:
- If a persona has `scope_exclusions`, scenarios for those areas MUST NOT be created
- The `create-scenario` skill will soft-warn if the requested scenario overlaps with a persona exclusion
- Empty default: `scope_exclusions: []` (always present, even when empty)

### 4.2 Scenario-level schema

Identical structure to persona-level. Position: After `implements_flows` array in scenario frontmatter.

```yaml
scope_exclusions:
  - area: "Brief description of the excluded behavior within this scenario"
    reason: technical | effort | business | strategic
    reason_detail: "Optional explanation"
    reconsider_in: "post-MVP"  # optional
```

**Use case for scenario-level**: A specific behavior within a scenario is excluded, but the
scenario itself exists. Example: The "Prepare Protocol for Client" scenario exists, but
"batch-generating protocols for multiple clients at once" is a scope exclusion within it.

**Empty default**: `scope_exclusions: []` (always present, even when empty)

## 9. Architecture Strategy

**Design decisions and rationale**:

**A. Soft warning in create-scenario (not hard block)**
The goal.md explicitly requires a soft warning. The user may intentionally override because:
- The scope exclusion may be outdated
- The new scenario may be narrower than the excluded area
- The user may want to reconsider the exclusion
A hard block would be paternalistic; a soft warning + user choice is correct.

**B. Empty array as default, not absent field**
Absent fields are invisible to AI agents. An explicit `scope_exclusions: []` is:
- Discoverable (agents see it and know the field exists)
- Prevents agents from writing scenarios for unstated exclusions
- Makes the "no exclusions" decision explicit rather than implicit

**C. Persona-level preferred over scenario-level**
As stated in goal.md: exclude early at persona level before scenarios are created.
Scenario-level is the fallback for late discoveries. README_17 must make this hierarchy clear.

**D. Consistent structure at both levels**
Same YAML schema at persona and scenario level reduces cognitive overhead. The same reason
taxonomy applies at both levels.

**E. README_17 explains relationship to README_14**
Scope exclusions are UPSTREAM of deviation documentation. The timing is:
- scope_exclusion: decided before any scenario/epic is written
- deviation: documented when an epic cannot fully implement an EXISTING need
These are complementary mechanisms at different stages of the requirements lifecycle.
